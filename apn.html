Scalable content that drives HIPAA compliant conversions for virtual IOP, APN's Lodge and in-patient care

The journey of a patient of All Points North is not linear. Deciding that you or a loved one needs care is often the hardest step. That's why we are proposing a non-linear paid media system that's designed to nurture potential patients, wherever they may be in their journey until they're ready to take that first step.

A dynamic system that's ready to scale

Bespoke and intentional creative

The creative is the most important part of our efforts. It drives the audience targeting, educates and pre-sells potential patients, and qualifies patients before they ever land on the website. Every piece is made for paid media, for one audience and one offer.

Landing page videos

A longer video on each landing page goes deeper than an ad can: what telehealth, the Lodge or the clinics are actually like, and the philosophy of care. The people who watch it are the ones worth staying in front of: educated, high intent, not ready yet.

Along the journey

Everyone is at a different step, and this takes time to process. People who already know APN don't get the introduction again, and when they're ready to take the next step, we're already there and they know where to go.

HIPAA compliant by design

Due to HIPAA we can't share PHI with Meta, which means we can't optimize for leads or booked appointments directly on the campaign. Instead, we have to send patient-blind data for further top of funnel events. Internally, we can track our campaign efficacy down to the patient and ensure that the PHI never leaves our systems.

What we'll produce

  • 24 ads: six for each space, meaning the Lodge, the Denver clinic, Boulder outpatient and telehealth, each delivered in three aspect ratios, 72 assets in all. Written for two audiences: the person in recovery and the loved one.
  • Three landing page videos: telehealth, the clinics and the Lodge.
  • Three landing pages, one for each offer, built to qualify a visitor before they're asked for anything personal, with inquiries routed to APN's contact center for placement. Delivered ready for APN's site.
  • An interview library of Noah and the clinicians, grown on the quarterly refresh days, so new ads keep coming between shoots.
  • Clinician interview clips, about five per speaker, cut from the interview day. Brand-agnostic, educational content for organic social and YouTube that reinforces the brand, and a supplement to the ad campaigns, especially retargeting.
  • A B-roll library of graded selects after every shoot. APN's to use anywhere, and ready-made for TikTok and LinkedIn.
  • HIPAA-safe conversion tracking and an attribution dashboard inside APN that connects ad spend to intake outcomes.

The library is the finished assets plus the interview footage and the B-roll, and it keeps growing through the monthly refreshes and the quarterly shoots.

Questions from the SOW

Most importantly, tell us what you believe APN should actually produce and why.
Ad creative for paid media and landing page videos to enhance the advertising funnel. An organic-first pipeline is good for gaining followers and driving social engagement, but there are fewer levers to steer those efforts and the production spend to drive leads. Paid media allows us to optimize for higher-intent actions, and with really intentional ad creative we can be very precise with our targeting and qualification before a user ever makes it to the landing page. Advertising also allows us to effectively test different messages, determine and scale winners, and further iterate on them. It also allows us to understand our patient acquisition cost clearly, so that we can make informed decisions about where to push and pull the various levers we have in the campaign.
What should we film first?
Telehealth, since we can advertise it nationally and it was mentioned to me on the call as the top priority. It also serves as a good test and blueprint for our conversion infrastructure, so that we can make any necessary tweaks and then roll out similar conversion infrastructure for the other campaigns. I also imagine the conversion cycle for telehealth will be on the longer side, so the sooner we can start educating and building a warm audience the better.
What is the telehealth launch plan?
We'll first get on a meeting to understand the exact customer journey for telehealth and what the customer contact process is like. I'll identify some key conversion events that we can target and build the skeleton for a campaign that optimizes for those specific events at different stages. I'll draft scripts that target the different personae that qualify for your telehealth services, six ads to start, with a second six scripted once we've learned from the first. If we are electing to do the landing page video, which I really recommend for telehealth especially, we will film that video and the ads consecutively. In the days between our initial meeting and our production we'll build our conversion infrastructure and landing page, and ensure that our systems are fully integrated with your current systems. When we launch the campaign we'll push people to a telehealth-focused landing page and optimize for 75% completion of the landing page video. Some of these users may convert via the lead form as well, but our initial goal is to build a warm and educated audience that we can focus our ad spend on. Once we have that audience built to 100,000 users we'll start optimizing for form start events, since we can't optimize for completed forms due to HIPAA regulations.
What can launch quickly, and what is the timeline to first content live?
A benchmark campaign on APN's existing collateral within the first three weeks, so there is a baseline before the library lands. The telehealth launch follows at week ten.
Whether and how would you leverage the November 13 Summit?
An optional shoot day. The clinician day covers APN's own people; what the Summit uniquely offers is the outside experts and referral partners in one room. A two-person crew led by an associate, short interviews with speakers around the program and never during it, and footage of the event and the Lodge that Jess can take to news outlets.
How much B-roll upfront, and what should be captured continuously?
We'll capture as much B-roll as we can up front during the initial six shoot days, and then continuously build the B-roll library on every additional filming engagement, including the quarterly refresh days.
What cadence for Noah and the clinicians?
We'd like to front-load as much of the production with them as possible, and we should only need a day of their time to do it. Who we film during the quarterly refreshes will depend on ad fatigue and performance signals. If Noah's ads perform far better than the other clinicians', for example, we'd want him for at least one of the quarterly refreshes. We'll let the metrics decide who we're filming, and we'll make it as easy as possible for them. It should not be a significant time commitment, probably one to three days a year at most, and we'll be very efficient with those days. Clinicians work from interview prompts with coaching on the day rather than heavy scripts. Scripted does not mean it will not be human, and I will never generate someone's face with AI.
How does performance shape later batches?
Performance drives what we film and what we script for the quarterly refresh shoots. Between those shoots, the monthly refreshes spin up new creative from the existing content library; the quarterly refreshes are additional production days with new scripts and targeted ad production. We'll keep iterating on what is working and test new ideas we haven't run before: move away from concepts that did not produce results, iterate further on the ones that did, and test new concepts we think could do better. The goal is always to meet the benchmark and exceed it, and the benchmark is set by the previous month's results. It's an iterative process.
How does the content connect to Meta advertising, creative testing, retargeting and conversion?
That is the entire goal of the content in this proposal. It's a departure from the SOW, but when I talked with Jess we agreed that paid advertising would be the priority here. Every single aspect of this content is designed to drive conversions on the landing page.
What about the organic-to-paid workflow in the SOW?
The clinician interview clips are the organic-to-paid pipeline in this proposal. We roll them out on social, see how they do, and add the ones that work into the ad campaigns, especially for retargeting. Someone sees an ad, goes to the page, and then experiences these interviews with the clinicians and becomes more sold on APN as a brand. They supplement the foundational ads; they're not a substitute for them.
Describe your approach to search and YouTube.
We look at trending keywords people are searching for on YouTube, working with your SEO partner, research what the competition for that keyword is currently publishing, and create expert-led videos that do everything that video is doing and more. The “and more” is the most important part: our expert is the better expert on that specific topic. A lot of this is testing thumbnails and titles and what actually gets people to click, then making a video engaging enough, by someone knowledgeable enough, to keep them watching the whole way through: animations, camera breaks, B-roll, and pacing that builds intrigue as the video goes, so the questions that come up while watching aren't answered until later. Most companies treat YouTube as an afterthought and put up the lowest common denominator, so it's usually possible to be the best video on a topic, but only if the content, the structure and the pacing are built intentionally. It's quality over quantity, which is why it's more expensive than the other production in this proposal. The upside is that these videos cut easily into short-form organic content that performs well, and they build a lot of trust for a brand. It's just a very separate effort from paid media.
Premium brand refresh: website hero video, flagship brand footage, photography, location imagery. Please price as a separate optional scope or future phase.
The landing page videos and the shoot days will give us enough footage to cut a piece that encompasses the whole brand. To tell the brand story in one homepage hero video we'd most likely film a separate video with Noah, and build it out with the clinician interviews and B-roll from the other shoots. I wouldn't make it the biggest priority. It's a really good thing to do about a year in, once the footage library is built up from all these other shoots. The homepage hero is priced above as an optional line.
